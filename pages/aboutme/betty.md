# The Triumph Herald

[Wikipedia describes the Triumph Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumph_Herald) as "a small two-door car introduced by Standard-Triumph of Coventry in 1959 and made through to 1971. The body design was by the Italian stylist Giovanni Michelotti, and the car was offered in saloon, convertible, coupé, estate and van models, with the latter marketed as the Triumph Courier."

Betty-Blue is the name my wife and I have given to the 1969 Triumph Herald 13/60 convertible that we have owned since September 2022. However, the love afair with the Herald started long before that, as we previously owned a White 1969 13/60, I dont recall the exact dates, but we owned it around 2009 until 2013. It was affectionately known as "Firdy" the reason being that it was an "F" reg, and it was something of an art to get it into 3rd gear! When we moved to Hong Kong we had to sell Firdy, and I cannot find any photographs of it. 
Roll forward a decade and I put a cheeky bid on eBay for a lovely looking Herald, not expecting to win it, but did :-) The only issue was the fact it was located ~250 miles away. Being the optimist I am, my wife and I drove the 4.5 hours to the area on the saturday, spent the night in a hotel and collected the Herald on Sunday morning to drive it home. Luckily it all went smoothly, if a little slower, with the return drive taking 10 hours as we chose to avoid all motorways. But the little car made it all the way just fine, a few minor back-fires which it turned out to be the Carb damper that needed lubricating.

Since every classic car is required to have a name, my wife christened this one Betty, and as its Blue in colour it seemed appropriate to be Betty-Blue.

## August 2025 respray
Although Betty was in reasonable condition, the paint work was a bit pitted in places and a silly spillage of fuel left a nasty paint buble patch around the filler cap, so we took the opportunity in August 2025 to give her a full body respray, and underseal and engine bay clean and make-over. All the chrome trim was removed and polished. She got a new horn (it had never worked since we got her!), full service, and a new water pump which had recently given up.
We discovered that originally Betty was a cream colour from the factory, not sure when she turned blue? but we decided to keep her blue, but this time we went with an original Triumph blue colour called "Wedgewood" (factory code 26). It was a slightly different shade of blue to what she had been, but barely noticable.
